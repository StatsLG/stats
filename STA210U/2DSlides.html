Pearson Correlation, \(r\)

Pearson Product-Moment Correlation Coefficient, \(r\) — describes the direction and strength of a linear relationship between two quantitative variables.

Important: \(r\) only describes the linear relationship. Another kind of relationship could still be present.

\[r=\frac{SXY}{\sqrt{SSX\,SSY}}\]

You will not need to calculate this formula by hand in this course.

Direction and Strength

\[-1\le r\le1\]

Positive

Larger X values tend to occur with larger Y values.

Negative

Larger values of one variable tend to occur with smaller values of the other.

Course strength rule

A relationship is generally considered strong when \(|r|\ge .75\).

Remember: \(r=0\) means no linear relationship—not necessarily no relationship at all.

What Does \(R^2\) Mean?

\(R^2\) — the percent of variation in Y explained by the model. In simple linear regression, \(R^2=r^2\).
\[0\%\le R^2\le100\%\]

Correlation Does Not Imply Causation

“Causal” Research — research whose objective is to determine whether one variable causes changes in another.
Correlation does not imply causation. It is never possible to prove causality just from the relationship between two variables.

Ice cream and assaults

A strong positive monthly correlation does not mean ice cream causes crime. Hot summer temperatures can increase both variables.

The variables are associated, but a third variable may explain the relationship.

Criteria for a Causal Relationship

1. Time order

The proposed cause must occur before the effect.

2. Co-variation

The graph and correlation must show a strong relationship.

3. Rationale

There must be a logical and compelling causal mechanism.

4. Non-spuriousness

Rival explanations must be ruled out.

The fourth criterion is especially difficult. Repeating studies under differing conditions can help rule out rival explanations.

Linear Regression

Regression — a technique used to predict variables based on other variables.
Linear Regression — predicts Y, the response variable, from X, the explanatory variable, using a linear equation.
\[\hat{Y}=b_0+b_1X\]
\(b_0\)
y-intercept
\(b_1\)
slope

Prediction is not exact. Error is represented by the vertical distance between a point and the regression line.

Method of Least Squares

Method of Least Squares — minimizes the sum of the squared vertical distances from the observed points to the regression line.
\[b_1=\frac{SXY}{SSX}\]
\[b_0=\bar{y}-b_1\bar{x}\]
Before fitting a line: the scatterplot and \(r\) should indicate that a linear model is reasonable.

Interpolation vs. Extrapolation

Interpolation

Predicting Y for X values within the observed range.

Extrapolation

Predicting Y for X values beyond the observed range.

In the drug example, X ranges from 100 to 500. Predicting at X = 1000 assumes the relationship continues beyond the observed data.

The Arithmetic Can Be Right—and the Prediction Wrong

\[\hat{Y}\approx25.22+2.61X\]

At age 30, the equation predicts about 103.5 inches—roughly 8 ft 7 in.

Key idea: the regression equation does not know when the real-world relationship changes. Extrapolation asks the model to make predictions where we collected no data.

People do not continue growing at the same linear rate after age 18, so extending the line far into adulthood is unreasonable.

  2. Interpret \(R^2\) in context.

Section 2D Recap

Correlation

\(r\) gives the direction and strength of a linear relationship.

\(R^2\)

The percent of variation in Y explained by the linear model.

Regression

\(\hat Y=b_0+b_1X\) predicts Y from X.

Cautions

Correlation ≠ causation. Avoid extrapolation. Inspect extreme values.
