STA210U — Section 2B

Descriptive Statistics: Numerical Descriptions for Univariate Data

In This Section

  • Measures of Central Tendency
  • Measures of Position
  • Measures of Dispersion (Variability)

1. Measures of Central Tendency

In this section we will mainly deal with statistics. Remember a statistic is a numerical characteristic of a sample. These are descriptive statistics since they summarize the data in the sample.

Measure of Central Tendency — a measure of average or typical value. The three most common measures are the mean, median, and mode.

Sample Mean

Sample Mean — when most people use the word average, they are talking about the mean.
\[\bar{x}=\frac{\sum x}{n}\]
  • \(\bar{x}\) is the sample mean
  • \(\sum\) means sum
  • \(x\) represents the data values
  • \(n\) is the sample size

The mean is the sum of the data values divided by the sample size.

Example 1

Select 4 students and ask, “How many brothers and sisters do you have?” Suppose the sample yields:

Data: 2, 3, 1, 3

Calculate the mean. Do you think the mean is a good measure of center for this data?

Show Answer
\[\bar{x}=\frac{2+3+1+3}{4}=\frac{9}{4}=2.25\]

The mean is 2.25 siblings. It is a reasonable description of center for this small data set, although 2.25 itself is not a possible number of siblings.

Example 2

Suppose we selected a 5th person who had 10 siblings.

New Data: 2, 3, 1, 3, 10

Calculate the mean. Do you think the mean is a good measure of center?

Show Answer
\[\bar{x}=\frac{19}{5}=3.8\]

The value 10 pulls the mean upward, illustrating that the mean is sensitive to extreme scores.

Important Characteristics of the Sample Mean

  • \(\bar{x}\) is sensitive to extreme scores.
  • \(\bar{x}\) is not necessarily a possible outcome.

An applied example of the mean not being used when extreme values are present is income. If you hear anyone talk about average income, they should say median income. A billionaire in a class would make the mean income very high and not a good measure of a typical value.

Sample Median

Sample Median — the middle score.

Procedure for calculating the sample median \(\tilde{x}\):

  • Rank the data from smallest to largest.
  • If \(n\) is odd, the median is the middle score.
  • If \(n\) is even, the median is the mean of the two middle scores.

Example 3

Data: 2, 3, 1, 3

Solve for the median.

Show Answer

Ordered data: 1, 2, 3, 3.

\[\tilde{x}=\frac{2+3}{2}=2.5\]

Example 4

New Data: 2, 3, 1, 3, 10

Solve for the median.

Show Answer

Ordered data: 1, 2, 3, 3, 10. The middle score is 3.

Important Characteristics of the Median

  • \(\tilde{x}\) is not sensitive to extreme scores.
  • Exactly half of the data is below the median and exactly half is above the median.

Because of the characteristics of the mean and median, if extreme scores exist in a data set the median is a better measure of central tendency. If extreme scores are unlikely, the mean varies less from sample to sample than the median and is a better measure of center.

Mean, Median, and Shape

The mean is sensitive to extreme values and is generally more affected by values farther from center. Therefore, if a distribution is skewed, the mean is pulled in the direction of the skewness. If there is no skewness, the mean and median will both be at the point of symmetry.

  • If \(\bar{x}>\tilde{x}\), the distribution is right skewed.
  • If \(\bar{x}<\tilde{x}\), the distribution is left skewed.
  • If \(\bar{x}=\tilde{x}\), the distribution is symmetric.

Sample Mode

Sample Mode — the most frequent score.

Example 5

Data: 2, 3, 1, 3
New Data: 2, 3, 1, 3, 10

Calculate the mode for both data sets.

Show Answer

The mode is 3 for both data sets.

There are major weaknesses with the mode. It may have more than one value, may not exist in a useful sense, and can change dramatically when only one observation changes. This makes it unstable.

The main advantage of the mode is that it can be used for qualitative data. For example, saying the “average” student in a class is female means that female is the most frequent category. The mean and median cannot be used for qualitative data.

Important Characteristics of the Mode

  • Does not always exist or can have more than one value.
  • Unstable.
  • Can be used with qualitative data.

2. Measures of Position

There are many measures of position. We will focus on quartiles, but first consider percentiles. If John scores in the 95th percentile in math, technically he scored better than 95% of the people who took the test. This does not necessarily tell how well he performed in absolute terms; it tells where his score is positioned relative to everyone else.

Quartiles — divide the data into four equally sized parts.

First Quartile, \(Q_1\)

Same as the 25th percentile.

25% of the data lies below \(Q_1\) and 75% lies above.

Second Quartile, \(Q_2\)

Same as the 50th percentile and also the median.

50% lies below and 50% lies above.

Third Quartile, \(Q_3\)

Same as the 75th percentile.

75% lies below \(Q_3\) and 25% lies above.

Procedure for Calculating Quartiles

  • Order the data from smallest to largest.
  • Find the median. This is \(Q_2\).
  • \(Q_1\) is the median of the lower half, not including \(Q_2\).
  • \(Q_3\) is the median of the upper half.
Five Number Summary — the low score, \(Q_1\), \(Q_2\), \(Q_3\), and the high score.

Example 6

Find the quartiles for each data set:

  • Data set #1: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
  • Data set #2: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7
  • Data set #3: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8
  • Data set #4: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9
Show Answers
  • #1: \(Q_1=2,\ Q_2=3.5,\ Q_3=5\)
  • #2: \(Q_1=2,\ Q_2=4,\ Q_3=6\)
  • #3: \(Q_1=2.5,\ Q_2=4.5,\ Q_3=6.5\)
  • #4: \(Q_1=2.5,\ Q_2=5,\ Q_3=7.5\)

Money-in-Pockets Example

In graduate school, a group of faculty and graduate students in a meeting were asked how much money they had in their pockets. The five-number summaries were:

GroupLow\(Q_1\)\(Q_2\)\(Q_3\)High
Students015710
Faculty1015253173

The value of 73 in the faculty data would be considered extreme.

3. Measures of Dispersion (Variability)

A measure of dispersion is a measure of the spread of the data points, or how much variability there is in the data set. Two distributions can have the same measures of center but still be very different because one has more variability.

The most common way to numerically describe a data set is with a measure of center and a measure of dispersion.

Sample Range

Sample Range — represents the distance between the high and low value.
\[\text{Range}=\text{High Score}-\text{Low Score}\]

Example 7

Years of experience for five faculty members: 1, 30, 22, 10, 5.

Calculate the range.

Show Answer
\[30-1=29\]

The range is 29 years.

Important Characteristics of the Range

  • Easy to compute.
  • Totally sensitive to extreme scores.
  • Not a good measure of dispersion in most cases.

Interquartile Range (IQR)

Interquartile Range (IQR) — measures the range of the middle 50% of the data.
\[\text{IQR}=Q_3-Q_1\]
  • Better than a typical range because it is not based on extreme values.
  • Still is not the best measure of dispersion in most cases.

Example 8

Using the pocket-money five-number summaries, calculate the IQR for students and faculty.

Show Answer

Students: \(7-1=6\)

Faculty: \(31-15=16\)

Sample Variance

Sample Variance — measures the average squared distance the data values are from \(\bar{x}\).

Conceptual formula:

\[s^2=\frac{\sum (x-\bar{x})^2}{n-1}\]

Algebraic formula:

\[s^2=\frac{n\sum x^2-(\sum x)^2}{n(n-1)}\]
  • \(s^2\) is the sample variance
  • \(\sum\) means sum
  • \(x\) represents the data values
  • \(\bar{x}\) is the sample mean
  • \(n\) is the sample size

The conceptual formula comes directly from the definition. The algebraic formula is equivalent and is often easier when calculating variance by hand.

Why Square the Distances?

If we calculate \(\sum(x-\bar{x})\), the result will always be zero. Values above the mean produce positive deviations and values below the mean produce negative deviations. Because the mean is the center, these balance out. Squaring prevents that cancellation.

Example 9

Data: 1, 30, 22, 10, 5

Calculate the variance.

Show Answer

\(n=5,\ \sum x=68,\ \sum x^2=1510\)

\[s^2=\frac{5(1510)-68^2}{5(4)}=\frac{2926}{20}=146.3\]

Important Characteristics of Variance

  • All data points are directly involved in the calculation.
  • Variance is in squared units, which makes it hard to interpret.

Sample Standard Deviation

Sample Standard Deviation — measures the average distance the data values are from \(\bar{x}\).

Variance is the average squared distance from the mean. To return to the original units of the data, we take the square root.

\[s=\sqrt{s^2}\]

Example 10

Data: 1, 30, 22, 10, 5

Calculate the standard deviation.

Show Answer
\[s=\sqrt{146.3}\approx12.10\]

The sample standard deviation is approximately 12.10 years.

Important Characteristics of Standard Deviation

  • Standard deviation uses the same units as the data.
  • Standard deviation is the most common measure of dispersion.
